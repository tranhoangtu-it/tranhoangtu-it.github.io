How Early and Mid-Stage Teams Actually Raise on Solana in 2026

A practical map of non-dilutive grants, Colosseum pre-seed, MetaDAO futarchy, and Series A capital on Solana.

# How Early and Mid-Stage Teams Actually Raise on Solana in 2026 A pattern I keep seeing among first-time technical founders is treating fundraising like a single pitch deck sprint: build a prototype for a month, DM thirty VC associates cold, and offer 15% of the company before a single real user has touched mainnet. On Solana, you rarely need to give up equity that early. Over the last few years, the capital stack around the ecosystem has split into distinct rails depending on where your product sits. Communities like @SuperteamUKR (which has already helped builders bring in over $1M across grants, hackathons, and bounties) act as an early filter and launchpad before institutional money ever gets involved. Below is a practical map of what early-stage and mid-stage fundraising on Solana looks like right now, including real check sizes, dilution terms, and examples of teams that took each route. ## 1. Zero-to-MVP: Non-Dilutive Grants ($1,000 to $100,000) If you have two or three builders and a working idea, giving away cap table space just to cover RPC bills, cloud hosting, and initial user testing is an expensive mistake. Two equity-free paths exist specifically for this stage: **Superteam Instagrants ($1,000 - $10,000 USDC)** These are fast, 100% non-dilutive microgrants meant for MVPs, developer tools, DePIN experiments, consumer apps, and local adoption work. Reviews through regional chapters like @SuperteamUKR usually take a week or two rather than months, and payouts are tied to straightforward shipping milestones. OOBE Protocol, for example, used a regional Superteam grant alongside an ideathon win to ship its early Solana agent runtime before expanding further. **Solana Foundation Milestone & Convertible Grants ($10,000 - $250,000)** If you are building open-source infrastructure, SDKs, validator tooling, or public goods under an MIT or Apache-2.0 license, standard milestone grants range from $10k up to $100k+ with zero equity taken (KYC and milestone sign-off required). For commercial startups building core ecosystem primitives, the Foundation also uses convertible grants ($50k-$250k) that stay non-dilutive unless the company later raises a priced venture round. ## 2. Prototype-to-Pre-Seed: Global Hackathons and Colosseum ($250,000) Historically, global Solana hackathons have produced more breakout startups than almost any cold VC outreach funnel. Colosseum now runs these global sprints paired with its own venture fund. Track winners take home $10k to $50k in non-dilutive prizes, and roughly 10 to 15 standout teams per cohort get accepted into the Colosseum Accelerator. The terms are standardized: $250,000 invested on a founder-friendly pre-seed SAFE plus token warrant (~7% ownership), six weeks of hands-on product mentorship from operators across the Solana stack, and a private Demo Day in front of active crypto funds. Teams like Tensor, Squads, StepN, Ore, and MagicBlock all used Solana hackathons to prove execution speed before raising larger rounds. ## 3. Early Traction Seed Rounds ($500,000 to $5,000,000) Once an app has real mainnet usage (consistent daily active wallets, sticky TVL, or measurable fee revenue), three different seed structures open up: **Curated Operator Angels and Community Platforms ($300k - $3M)** Platforms like Echo and Legion, alongside Solana founder syndicates (operators from Helius, Jupiter, Backpack, and Jito), let teams raise from people who actually use and integrate the product. Terms usually mirror lead seed SAFE + token side letters ($15M to $45M valuation caps) or merit-weighted community allocations. **Market-Governed Raises on MetaDAO ($500k - $5M+)** Unique to Solana, MetaDAO lets teams raise USDC directly into an onchain treasury governed by conditional prediction markets (futarchy). Instead of trusting a PDF roadmap, token holders govern monthly team budgets and major strategic proposals onchain, giving early backers real downside protection while letting founders skip six-month VC roadshows. Teams like mtnCapital and Future proved this model works with real seven-figure treasuries. **Institutional Seed Funds ($2M - $6M)** Funds active at seed on Solana (Multicoin, Colosseum, Frictionless, Archetype, Solana Ventures, Framework, Anagram, 6th Man) typically lead $2M to $6M rounds for 10% to 15% dilution via Post-Money SAFE + Token Warrant or SAFT, with a standard 1-year cliff and 24 to 36 months of linear vesting. MagicBlock, for instance, raised $3M at pre-seed followed by a $7.5M seed led by Lightspeed Faction after demonstrating sub-50ms ephemeral rollups. ## 4. Mid-Stage Growth & Series A ($8,000,000 to $25,000,000+) At mid-stage, narrative alone stops working. Institutional Series A and B investors look at annualized fee capture, liquidity depth, security audits, and regulatory or technical moats. Typical dilution sits around 10% to 18% at valuations from $60M to $200M+ with strict multi-year lockups. A few concrete Solana benchmarks show what mid-stage traction looks like in practice: - **Squads Labs:** Solved multisig and smart-account security for Solana teams first, grew to secure billions onchain, and raised a $5.7M strategic round followed by a $10M Series A led by Electric Capital. - **Jito Labs:** Focused on MEV client infrastructure, captured the majority of Solana staked weight, and closed a $10M Series A co-led by Multicoin Capital and Framework Ventures. - **Backpack:** Combined wallet distribution (Mad Lads & xNFTs) with regulated exchange licenses to raise a $17M Series A at a $120M valuation led by Placeholder VC. - **Drift Protocol:** Scaled decentralized perpetuals volume through bear and bull cycles, raising a $3.8M seed from Multicoin and later a $23.5M Series B led by Polychain Capital. ## Practical Sequencing for Founders 1. Ship code before pitching equity. Plug into a local builder hub like @SuperteamUKR, win a few Earn bounties or ship a clean devnet repo, and use a $5k-$10k Instagrant to get your first hundred real users at 0% dilution. 2. Treat Colosseum hackathons as your public audition. Even teams that miss first prize routinely meet their seed lead through hackathon judging. 3. Split public goods from commercial monetization where it makes sense: open-source client SDKs can qualify for Foundation grants while your hosted API or protocol fees fund the business. 4. Pick mid-stage capital based on what bottleneck you actually have. If you need CEX market makers, enterprise intros, or licensing, institutional VCs earn their allocation. If your edge is retail liquidity and community alignment, Echo, Legion, or MetaDAO will serve your cap table better.
